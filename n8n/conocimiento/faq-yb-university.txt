== Getting Started ==

Q: What exactly is YB University?
A: YB University is a professional learning initiative within the Palm Beach Yacht Sales ecosystem. It introduces yacht brokerage through industry education, practical exercises, and pathways for further exploration.

Q: Who is YB University for?
A: For students, career explorers, working professionals, creators, and entrepreneurs interested in understanding the yachting industry. You do not need an established marine-industry background to begin.

Q: Do I need previous boating or yacht sales experience?
A: No. Yacht Brokerage Professional Foundations is designed to start with core terminology, vessel knowledge, professional roles, and brokerage processes.

Q: Do I need a college degree?

Q: Do I need to own a yacht or know wealthy people?
A: No. Learning yacht brokerage does not require yacht ownership or an existing network. Industry knowledge, professionalism, and relationships can be developed over time.

Q: Is there a minimum age to participate?

Q: Can international students participate?
A: Online participation may be possible from eligible locations, subject to program availability and applicable restrictions. The course does not grant immigration status, work authorization, or a professional license.

== The Industry ==

Q: What does a yacht broker actually do?
A: A yacht broker assists clients buying and selling yachts. Work may include vessel research, marketing, showings, offers, negotiations, surveys, and coordination with other professionals.

Q: Is yacht brokerage only about selling luxury yachts?
A: No. The industry spans different vessel categories and price ranges. The wider marine ecosystem also includes operations, technology, hospitality, marketing, and services.

Q: Is yacht brokerage a realistic career opportunity?
A: It can be a professional path for qualified, persistent people, but outcomes depend on market conditions, experience, licensing, and individual effort. No job or income is guaranteed.

Q: Do yacht brokers earn commissions?
A: Many brokers receive transaction-based commissions. Compensation, brokerage splits, expenses, taxes, and timing vary. Gross commission is not the same as personal take-home income.

Q: Can I work in yachting without becoming a broker?
A: Yes. Related roles include marketing, digital media, sales support, client service, marina operations, technology, administration, and marine services. Qualifications vary by role.

Q: Is the yacht industry difficult to enter?
A: Entry can be challenging without industry knowledge or contacts. The program is designed to explain professional expectations and help learners identify practical next steps, not remove every barrier.

Q: Is technology and AI changing yacht brokerage?
A: Digital listings, CRM systems, analytics, marketing tools, and AI-assisted workflows are changing many business processes. Human judgment, privacy, and accountability remain essential.
